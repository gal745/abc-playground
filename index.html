How to play together

  • Sound on! The games talk, so no reading is needed. Tap 🔊 to hear it again, 🏠 to go back.
  • Levels: every game has 5 levels. Finish a level to unlock the next one and fill a dot on the game's button.
  • 🎈 Balloon Pop: listen for the letter and pop the balloons that show it. At level 3 the letter is hidden, so listen for the sound! Levels 6 and 7 use the sound pairs — sh ch th oo ee ck — with the pair hidden on the last one.
  • 🐶 First Sounds: say the picture's name slowly, stretching the first sound (“sssun”), then tap the letter it starts with.
  • 🃏 Letter Match: turn over two cards to find a capital letter and its small letter.
  • 🎵 Rhyme Time: say the words out loud. Which one sounds the same at the end?
  • 🧱 Build a Word: tap the letters in order as you hear each sound, then blend them: c‑a‑t → cat!
  • 🔢 Number Words: count the pictures, then spell the number word by tapping its letters in order. Say each letter name together.
  • 🦖 Feed the Dino: the dino asks for a sound. Feed it a picture that starts with that sound.
  • ✏️ Trace It: trace the big letter with a finger. Tap the sponge to start again.
  • 🚂 Alphabet Train: say the alphabet together and fill in the missing letters.
  • 📅 Days of the Week: the ending day is already written, so only the beginning is spelled: Mon‑day. Say the days in order together. At level 5 the day isn't named — work out which one comes next.
  • 🔎 Which Word?: real reading. The words look alike — cat, hat, bat — so the child has to look carefully at the letters, not guess from the picture. The picture only appears once they are right. Level 5 changes just the vowel: pan, pin, pen.
  • 🎯 Middle Sound: the trickiest sound in a short word is the vowel in the middle. Say the word slowly together — c…a…t — and listen for the sound between the first and the last.
  • 🧲 Join the Sounds: today's one. Tap each letter to hear its sound, then push them together and the sounds run into a word: a + t → at, c + a + t → cat. The 🧲 Joining sounds poster on the home screen has the same thing to point at, and prints for the wall.
  • 🤝 Sound Pairs: some sounds need two letters — sh ch th oo ee, and now ck as well. The pair sits on one card, so shop is three sounds, not four. The 🤝 Sound pairs poster has them all with words, and prints for the wall.
  • 🧩 Finish the Word: the pair is missing — __ip, m__n — and they choose the two letters that belong. Say the word slowly together first.
  • ✂️ Worksheets: eight printable sheets for pencil work (circle the pair, write the missing pair, draw a line, one box per sound, tick what you hear, and three sheets of joining letters — they use different words from each other, and the last one ends with four-sound words). Make new sheets picks different words, so you can print a fresh set each week.
  • 🐉 Feed the Dragon: the dino grown up. The dragon wants a sound pair — at the start of a word, then anywhere in it, then at the end — and on the last two levels it asks for a word by name, so the choices have to be read. After each one the whole word is written out under the dragon, with each piece lighting up as it is sounded out, so the word is seen as well as heard.
  • 🐛 Change a Sound: the caterpillar says a word, and one sound has to change to make a different one — cat to hat, cap to cup, shop to chop. The new word is only ever said out loud, never written, so your child has to hear which sound is different and choose the letter that makes it. The first three levels say which sound changes (first, last, middle); after that they have to find it themselves. Five changes and the caterpillar turns into a butterfly.
  • ☁️ Catch the Cloud: a sound is given, and clouds drift past carrying the little words. Catch the clouds whose word has that sound in it — at the start, then at the end, then in the middle, then the pairs sh ch th oo ee ck. Every word is two or three letters, so each cloud can be read before it is caught. On the last level the sound is only heard, not shown.
  • 🌱 Grow a Sentence: for a child who freezes when a whole sentence lands at once. It arrives a piece at a time — a cat, then a cat on a bed — and nothing can be got wrong while it grows; the question only comes when the whole thing has been read in bits. Later levels grow it word by word, and the last one gives the whole sentence at once for when they are ready.
  • 🧺 b and d Sort: the same decision as b or d? the other way round — the letter is already in the word and it goes in the blue b basket or the red d basket. Just the letter first, then words that start with it, then words that end with it. Short, quick rounds, because this one is fixed by repetition. The sixth level shows no word at all — it is only said, so they have to hear the first sound and pick the basket.
  • 🔍 b and d Hunt: a whole board of b's and d's, and only one of the two is wanted — find every one of them and the other ones have to be left alone. This is the plainest practice there is for telling the two shapes apart, and it is quick. The first two levels are single letters, then the board fills with words that start with the letter, then words with it anywhere, and the last one is a big board against the clock.
  • 🐝 b Words: a bag to fill with every word on the board that starts with b. The words are ones your child already knows how to say — banana, butterfly, balloon, bath — not the ones that happen to be easy to sound out, so the picture carries the meaning and the only question is the first sound. Each level is one kind of thing: animals, then food, then toys, then things at home, then all of them mixed on a bigger board. From level 2 there are d words on the board to leave alone. A wrong tap names the sound that word really starts with ("hen starts with h — we want b, like bee") and leaves the card there to try again. Every word found drops into the bag at the top, so at the end of a round they can see the whole bunch they collected.
  • 🦆 d Words: the same game for d — dog, duck, donut, doll, door — with b words among the ones to leave alone. Two separate games on purpose: one letter at a time is much easier than swapping between them, so they can do a round of b today and a round of d tomorrow.
  • 🧺 Pack the Picnic: the last level of Feed the Dino, which is the one they ask for, made into a game of its own — and nothing is being eaten. A sound is given and two of the foods on the screen start with it. That matters: one right answer can be a lucky guess, two cannot. Each one found flies into the basket and stays there with its name, so the round ends with pancake and peach, both starting with p. Four pictures first, then five, then a level where the letter is never shown and only the sound is said, then six, and the last one asks for three, listening. A wrong pick says what that thing starts with instead of just buzzing.
  • 🎒 Pack your Bag: the same game with toys and things from round the house instead of food — kite, key, candle, crayons. Two games rather than one so the words stay in a world they know while the job stays the same.
  • 🛏️ b or d?: for the mix-up every child has. A word is missing one letter and the choice is always the same two, so the only question is which way it faces: _og, be_, we_. b is always blue — b for blue — and d is always red, on the buttons, in the word and on the bed card that stays on the screen: blue b at the front of bed, red d at the back, which is the shape of a bed. A wrong answer names what they picked by colour and word ("that is the red one, dee, like dog"). Start of the word, then the end, then either; level 4 says nothing at all and level 5 only says the word.
  • 📘 Read and Find: today's lesson as a game. The word is shown and nothing says it — your child reads it, then taps the picture it means. Tapping the word sounds it out, for when they are stuck, and a wrong picture gets the word read out before they try again. Two pictures, then three, then a whole family (cat, hat, bat — only the first letter tells them apart), then the pairs, then four-sound words.
  • 🖼️ Match the Sentence: a whole sentence to read — a cat up a tree — and the pictures it tells you about. Tap the sentence to hear it if they need it. Level 3 puts one word of difference between the choices, and level 5 gives no help at all.
  • 🫧 Word Bubbles: Balloon Pop for whole words. The bubbles carry words, not letters, so each one has to be read before it is popped. The word is never written on the card — it is heard, or shown as a picture — so this really is reading. Two-letter words first, then three, then the pairs, two levels where nothing is said at all, and a sixth level of four-sound words (frog, tent, drum) from today's lesson.
  • 🐸 Feed the Frog: like Feed the Dino, but the frog eats a sentence. Flies drift past carrying words and it will only take them in order, so a cat up a tree gets built one word at a time. Tap the frog to hear the sentence again. The sentences are the same ones as the last worksheet of Today's lesson.
  • 📖 Today's lesson: a short reading lesson a child can do on their own — about twenty-five minutes, eight parts, each one ticking itself off. This one keeps on with b and d, the mix-up they keep hitting, but in words they already know how to say: the two letters with the bed reminder, then animals (is it a bear or a dog — which sound does it start with?), then food and toys. Part 4 leaves the screen: it says, out loud, to put the tablet down and read a real book with you, and the page goes quiet and waits until the child taps to say the book is finished — it is numbered like all the other parts, so it reads as part of the lesson rather than a break from it. Then back to reading: words with a letter missing, then three- and four-letter words that carry a b or a d — bed, bug, web, bath, sled, duck — read whole with nothing said first and a picture to pick, then a long word taken a bit at a time, and a sentence that grows a piece at a time rather than landing all at once.
  • ✏️ Lesson sheets: the longer version to do together: ten worksheets done on the screen, about an hour altogether — warming up the sounds, joining two and three letters, finding and filling in the pairs sh ch th oo ee, reading words that look alike, word families, then a bank of every word read in the lesson and short sentences built out of those same words (nothing longer than four letters). The page says every sound and ticks each sheet off as it is finished. There is a clock to pace the hour, and 🎲 New words gives that sheet a fresh set, so the same lesson works again next week.
  • If a letter looks like a word (an e showing as and, an i as in): the browser is translating the page. Tap the translate icon in the address bar and choose Never translate this site, or turn translation off in the browser’s settings. The games already ask browsers not to translate them, but a browser set to always translate English will do it anyway.
  • Tips: 5–10 minutes is plenty. Let your child tap, and praise trying, not just right answers.

🔊 Sound check: hear every sound

Computer voices can only roughly say letter sounds. Open Sound check to hear every one, and to change any that come out wrong — each sound is a spelling the voice reads, so a different spelling fixes it. If a sound still isn't clear, say it yourself too. To use your own voice, record files named a.mp3, b.mp3… and put them in the audio/sounds folder.
