How to play together

  • Sound on! The games talk, so no reading is needed. Tap 🔊 to hear it again, 🏠 to go back.
  • Levels: every game has 5 levels. Finish a level to unlock the next one and fill a dot on the game's button.
  • 🎈 Balloon Pop: listen for the letter and pop the balloons that show it. At level 3 the letter is hidden, so listen for the sound! Levels 6 and 7 use the sound pairs — sh ch th oo ee — with the pair hidden on the last one.
  • 🐶 First Sounds: say the picture's name slowly, stretching the first sound (“sssun”), then tap the letter it starts with.
  • 🃏 Letter Match: turn over two cards to find a capital letter and its small letter.
  • 🎵 Rhyme Time: say the words out loud. Which one sounds the same at the end?
  • 🧱 Build a Word: tap the letters in order as you hear each sound, then blend them: c‑a‑t → cat!
  • 🔢 Number Words: count the pictures, then spell the number word by tapping its letters in order. Say each letter name together.
  • 🦖 Feed the Dino: the dino asks for a sound. Feed it a picture that starts with that sound.
  • ✏️ Trace It: trace the big letter with a finger. Tap the sponge to start again.
  • 🚂 Alphabet Train: say the alphabet together and fill in the missing letters.
  • 📅 Days of the Week: the ending day is already written, so only the beginning is spelled: Mon‑day. Say the days in order together. At level 5 the day isn't named — work out which one comes next.
  • 🔎 Which Word?: real reading. The words look alike — cat, hat, bat — so the child has to look carefully at the letters, not guess from the picture. The picture only appears once they are right. Level 5 changes just the vowel: pan, pin, pen.
  • 🎯 Middle Sound: the trickiest sound in a short word is the vowel in the middle. Say the word slowly together — c…a…t — and listen for the sound between the first and the last.
  • 🧲 Join the Sounds: today's one. Tap each letter to hear its sound, then push them together and the sounds run into a word: a + t → at, c + a + t → cat. The 🧲 Joining sounds poster on the home screen has the same thing to point at, and prints for the wall.
  • 🤝 Sound Pairs: some sounds need two letters — sh ch th oo ee. The pair sits on one card, so shop is three sounds, not four. The 🤝 Sound pairs poster has them all with words, and prints for the wall.
  • 🧩 Finish the Word: the pair is missing — __ip, m__n — and they choose the two letters that belong. Say the word slowly together first.
  • ✂️ Worksheets: eight printable sheets for pencil work (circle the pair, write the missing pair, draw a line, one box per sound, tick what you hear, and three sheets of joining letters — they use different words from each other, and the last one ends with four-sound words). Make new sheets picks different words, so you can print a fresh set each week.
  • 🐉 Feed the Dragon: the dino grown up. The dragon wants a sound pair — at the start of a word, then anywhere in it, then at the end — and on the last two levels it asks for a word by name, so the choices have to be read. After each one the whole word is written out under the dragon, with each piece lighting up as it is sounded out, so the word is seen as well as heard.
  • 🐛 Change a Sound: the caterpillar says a word, and one sound has to change to make a different one — cat to hat, cap to cup, shop to chop. The new word is only ever said out loud, never written, so your child has to hear which sound is different and choose the letter that makes it. The first three levels say which sound changes (first, last, middle); after that they have to find it themselves. Five changes and the caterpillar turns into a butterfly.
  • ☁️ Catch the Cloud: a sound is given, and clouds drift past carrying the little words. Catch the clouds whose word has that sound in it — at the start, then at the end, then in the middle, then the pairs sh ch th oo ee. Every word is two or three letters, so each cloud can be read before it is caught. On the last level the sound is only heard, not shown.
  • 🫧 Word Bubbles: Balloon Pop for whole words. The bubbles carry words, not letters, so each one has to be read before it is popped. The word is never written on the card — it is heard, or shown as a picture — so this really is reading. Two-letter words first, then three, then the pairs, and on the last two levels nothing is said at all.
  • 🐸 Feed the Frog: like Feed the Dino, but the frog eats a sentence. Flies drift past carrying words and it will only take them in order, so a cat up a tree gets built one word at a time. Tap the frog to hear the sentence again. The sentences are the same ones as the last worksheet of Today's lesson.
  • 📝 Today's lesson: ten worksheets done on the screen, about an hour altogether — warming up the sounds, joining two and three letters, finding and filling in the pairs sh ch th oo ee, reading words that look alike, word families, then a bank of every word read in the lesson and short sentences built out of those same words (nothing longer than four letters). The page says every sound and ticks each sheet off as it is finished. There is a clock to pace the hour, and 🎲 New words gives that sheet a fresh set, so the same lesson works again next week.
  • If a letter looks like a word (an e showing as and, an i as in): the browser is translating the page. Tap the translate icon in the address bar and choose Never translate this site, or turn translation off in the browser’s settings. The games already ask browsers not to translate them, but a browser set to always translate English will do it anyway.
  • Tips: 5–10 minutes is plenty. Let your child tap, and praise trying, not just right answers.

Voice

Tip: for the most natural, child-friendly voices on a computer, open the games in Microsoft Edge. Phones and tablets already have good voices.

🔊 Sound check: hear every sound

Computer voices can only roughly say letter sounds. Open Sound check to hear every one, and to change any that come out wrong — each sound is a spelling the voice reads, so a different spelling fixes it. If a sound still isn't clear, say it yourself too. To use your own voice, record files named a.mp3, b.mp3… and put them in the audio/sounds folder.
